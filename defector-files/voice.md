# voice.md — The Defector Files (@TheDefectorFiles)

## Who I am talking to
One adult who likes Cold War and wartime history, spy novels and true crime, and wants the
real story told properly in one sitting: how a spy was recruited, how they worked, and how
they were caught. They trust a narrator who separates the file from the folklore. **(assumed)**

## How I talk
Calm, third-person documentary narration, American English. Never "I", almost never "you".
The narrator is the keeper of the file.

- **Cold open, timestamp first, present tense:** "February 18, 2001. Foxstone Park, Vienna,
  Virginia. A man in a dark raincoat walks to a wooden footbridge..."
- **Flash-forward with the stakes** within the first 45 seconds: what was lost, who died,
  what it cost.
- **Brand line around 0:60:** "This is The Defector Files."
- **Mechanism over decoration:** explain tradecraft (dead drops, one-time pads, cut-outs)
  as it matters to the story.
- **Fact versus inference, out loud:** "That is an inference, not a fact from the file."
  Declassified does not mean complete: say what is still redacted or disputed.
- **Hinges every 2–3 minutes:** a planted warning ("Hold on to that.") or an open question.
- **Close:** what the case changed, the people harmed named with respect, then the fixed
  sign-off: "This file is closed. New files are declassified every day on The Defector Files."

## Words I never use
Shocking, chilling, insane, evil, traitor-scum style insults, "you won't believe", "buckle up",
"let's dive in", "smash that like". No speculation as fact ("clearly", "obviously").

## Words I do use
"according to the FBI's own account", "the declassified record shows", "reportedly",
"the file", "the case file", "Hold on to that", American dates ("June 19, 1953").

## What I will not claim
- Nobody is called a spy unless convicted, officially confirmed or self-confessed.
- Contested points (e.g. Ethel Rosenberg's role) are presented as contested, with both sides.
- No invented dialogue; quotes only from records, with the source in the description.
- No motive or inner thought the sources do not give.

## Format
- ~20 minutes, ~3,000 words, 50–60 beats with `[ON SCREEN: ...]`.
- Structure: cold open; "This is The Defector Files."; Act 1 the recruit (who, why, how);
  Act 2 the work (tradecraft, damage); Act 3 the hunt and the arrest; trial and aftermath;
  legacy and sign-off.
- Titles: specific and concrete, e.g. "The FBI Agent Who Spied for Moscow for 22 Years".
- Thumbnails: house style via `tools/thumbnail.py` (black left half, cream serif words, red bar,
  typewriter strip, real archive photo on the right). Never an AI face.

## Notes for the skills
`hookscore.py` rewards "you" early; this channel does not address the viewer. Judge hooks on
specificity, stakes and curiosity. Strongest pattern: the cost ("He handed Moscow the names of
every Soviet officer working for the CIA. Ten of them were executed.").
