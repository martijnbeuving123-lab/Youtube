## Timeline and facts

### Sebold, the recruit
- Born Gottlieb Adolf Wilhelm Sebold, Mülheim, March 10, 1899. German army engineering corps in WWI.
  Emigrated to the US 1922; worked in industrial and aircraft plants in the US and South America.
  Naturalized February 10, 1936.
- February 1939: returns to Germany to visit his mother in Mülheim. In Hamburg a Gestapo man tells
  him he will be contacted because of what he knows about US aircraft plants.
- September 1939: "Dr. Gassner" visits him in Mülheim, asks about US military planes and asks him to
  go back as an agent. Gassner and "Dr. Renken" threaten to expose that he omitted a German prison
  term from his US naturalization application. "Renken" = Major Nikolaus Ritter, Abwehr (Hamburg,
  air intelligence). Threats also to his family. Sebold agrees.
- Seven weeks of training in Hamburg: clandestine shortwave radio, codes, microphotographs. Alias
  "Harry Sawyer", code name TRAMP, Abwehr number A.3549.
- Before leaving Germany he goes to the US Consulate in Cologne, insists on seeing the Consul
  General, says he was blackmailed and is a loyal American who wants to work with the FBI.
- Sails from Genoa; arrives New York February 8, 1940 (FBI/Batvinis: the case started for the FBI
  in February 1940).

### The FBI's set-up
- Office as a consulting diesel engineer, "Diesel Research Company", in Times Square, Manhattan.
  Hidden microphones and a two-way mirror; FBI agents filmed the meetings from the next room
  (⚠ room numbers 627/628 from a single secondary source; not used).
- May 1940: FBI agents set up a shortwave station on Long Island (⚠ Centerport, near Huntington,
  per secondary sources; said in narration only as "on Long Island"), built by FBI laboratory
  engineers, posing as Sebold's transmitter. It ran 16 months: over 300 messages sent to Germany
  (FBI-vetted, false or useless information), about 200 received from Germany.
- Through Sebold the FBI identified German agents in the US, Mexico and South America.
- Special Agent James Ellsworth shadowed Sebold for the whole 16 months. Agent Raymond Newkirk
  ("Ray McManus") rented the room above Duquesne's apartment near Central Park with a hidden
  microphone. Newkirk on Duquesne's counter-surveillance: "He would take a local train, change to
  an express, change back to a local, go through a revolving door and keep going on right around..."
  Duquesne told Sebold he was sure he was watched and once confronted an agent.
- First meeting: Duquesne feared microphones in the office, handed Sebold a note suggesting they
  talk elsewhere; they moved to an automat.

### Fritz Duquesne, code name DUNN
- Born East London, Cape Colony, September 21, 1877 (Boer family of Huguenot origin). Boer officer
  in the Second Boer War; captured by the British three times and by the Portuguese once, escaped
  each time (⚠ his own and his biographers' account; Ronnie treats much of his legend as
  self-invented). Sent to Bermuda as a POW, escaped to the US; naturalized 1913.
- Between the wars: journalist (New York Herald), lecturer on big game, claimed adviser to Theodore
  Roosevelt; c. 30 known aliases. In 1910 involved, with Burnham and Congressman Broussard, in a
  plan to import hippopotamuses into Louisiana to ease a US meat shortage.
- WWI: German agent; sabotaged British ships in South America with bombs hidden in cargo (FBI
  write-up as summarised). Arrested in New York November 17, 1917 for insurance fraud; feigned
  paralysis for nearly two years in Bellevue prison ward; escaped May 1919 dressed as a woman (⚠
  details vary).
- Claimed to have sunk HMS *Hampshire* with Kitchener aboard (June 1916). ⚠ Historians: *Hampshire*
  hit a mine laid by U-75; Duquesne's claim is unsupported. The 1932 book title *The Man Who Killed
  Kitchener* comes from his own legend.
- 1932: arrested again in New York (murder on the high seas), released when Britain declined to
  pursue. 1934 intelligence officer for the pro-Nazi Order of 76; 1935 worked for the WPA.
- Canaris knew him from WWI; Ritter (friend since 1931) reconnected with him in New York December 3,
  1937. Ritter's instruction to Sebold: contact DUNN.
- To Sebold: photographs and specifications of a new bomb (claimed to have entered the DuPont plant
  in Wilmington, Delaware ⚠ his claim); explained how to start fires in industrial plants. Most of
  his material came from letters to companies, posing as a writer/student. Letter to the Chemical
  Warfare Service asking about a new gas mask: "Don't be concerned if this information is
  confidential, because it will be in the hands of a good, patriotic citizen." The reply was read in
  Berlin about a week later (⚠ as the FBI tells it).
- Sentence: 18 years espionage + 2 concurrent years and $2,000 fine (Foreign Agents Registration
  Act). Leavenworth; beaten by inmates; released 1954 for ill health; died indigent at City Hospital,
  Welfare Island, May 24, 1956, aged 78.

### The ring (33 convicted)
- Hermann Lang: inspector at Carl L. Norden Corp. In 1937 copied Norden bombsight drawings at his
  kitchen table at night; January 9, 1938 handed them, hidden in a wooden umbrella casing, to a
  courier on SS *Reliance* bound for Bremen. Paid $1,500; invited to Germany to help rebuild it;
  received by Göring. 18 years; deported 1950. ⚠ How much Germany used the Norden design is
  disputed (Wikipedia: later Luftwaffe sights similar gyro platform; Lotfe 7 independent design).
  Key: the theft happened in 1937–38, before Sebold's case began.
- Everett Roeder (born in the Bronx; Sperry Gyroscope engineer): radio instrumentation of a new
  Glenn Martin bomber, range finders, blind-flying instruments, Lockheed Hudson wiring; 16 guns at
  his Long Island home. 16 years.
- Lilly Stein (Vienna-born, trained at Hamburg; worked as artist's model in New York): 10 years.
- Rene Mezenen, Pan Am clipper steward, courier New York–Lisbon in 24 hours; reported convoys.
- Couriers among ship crews (Siegler, Stigler, Strunck, Fehse); Strunck asked to push a British
  officer overboard and take his diplomatic bag; Sebold talked him out of it.
- Leo Waalen: per prosecutors, passed the sailing date of SS *Robin Moor* (sunk by U-69, May 21,
  1941; all aboard survived after days in lifeboats) for transmission five days before it sailed
  (⚠ trial testimony; we do not claim his report caused the sinking).
- Paul Bante brought dynamite and detonators to Sebold's office.
- Japanese naval officer Takeo Ezima filmed meeting Sebold; left the US on the *Kamakura Maru*.

### Arrests, trial, sentences
- Arrests: end of June 1941 (FBI: 33 agents; the big sweep June 28–29, 1941). The FBI print of
  Duquesne in the Sawyer office is dated June 25, 1941.
- 19 pleaded guilty. Trial of 14 in Federal District Court, Brooklyn, from September 3, 1941; the
  FBI films were shown to the jury. All 14 guilty on December 13, 1941 (six days after Pearl Harbor).
- Sentenced January 2, 1942: over 300 years in total.
- A German spymaster later called the roundup "the death blow" to German espionage in the US.
  Hoover: "the greatest spy roundup in U.S. history". Film *The House on 92nd Street* (1945) is a
  thinly disguised version.
- After the war: when the US entered the war, the FBI was confident no major German network
  remained. In June 1942 the Abwehr landed saboteurs by U-boat instead (Operation Pastorius).
- Sebold: new identity, chicken farm in California; constant fear of revenge; committed to Napa
  State Hospital 1965 (manic depression); died there February 16, 1970, aged 70. First told in Alan
  Hynd, *Passport to Treason* (1943).
- Ritter's other double-agent failure: Arthur Owens (SNOW) in Britain worked for MI5. Ritter was
  moved out of the Abwehr's agent work in 1942.

## Angle (originality rule 1)
The ring's greatest coup, the Norden bombsight, had already left New York in an umbrella in January
1938, two years before the FBI's case began. So the case is not the story of a secret saved; it is
the story of how the FBI, for the first time, ran an enemy's own channel: the Abwehr coerced a
reluctant recruit, he walked into a consulate, and for 16 months Hamburg talked to an FBI radio.
And the man who made it possible ended his life afraid and forgotten while the legend went to
Hoover and to Duquesne.

## Contested / handle with care
- Duquesne's Boer-war escapes, Kitchener claim, Roosevelt connection: his own legend; flagged.
- "Largest espionage case ending in convictions in US history": FBI's own claim.
- How damaging the ring was: Norden theft predates the case; FBI controlled most later traffic.
- Lilly Stein: the "nymphomaniac" FBI quote is not used (demeaning, single source).
