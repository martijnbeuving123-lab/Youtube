## Legal position (hard limits)
- Smith was **tried for one murder only: Bessie Mundy** (Old Bailey, 22 Jun – 1 Jul 1915),
  convicted, appeal dismissed by the Court of Criminal Appeal (Lord Reading LCJ), hanged at
  Maidstone on **13 Aug 1915**. He was charged with all three deaths on 23 Mar 1915, but under
  English law could be tried on one indictment; the deaths of **Alice Burnham** and
  **Margaret Lofty** were admitted as evidence of "system". Script wording: he was convicted
  of Bessie's murder; the other two deaths were put before the jury as part of a pattern.
  Nobody else is accused of anything.

## People
- **George Joseph Smith** (11 Jan 1872, Bethnal Green – 13 Aug 1915). Son of an insurance
  agent. Reformatory at Gravesend at 9; later prison for theft and fraud; 1896, 12 months for
  persuading a woman to steal from her employers. Aliases include George Oliver Love, George
  Rose Smith, Henry Williams, Charles Oliver James, John Lloyd.
- **Caroline Beatrice Thornhill** — married him 1898 in Leicester as "George Oliver Love";
  his only legal marriage. Stole for him as a maid; caught in Worthing; later informed on him
  (he got two years, 1901); emigrated to Canada.
- **Edith Pegler** (Wikipedia spells "Peglar") — married 30 Jul 1908 in Bristol after
  answering an advertisement for a housekeeper; the woman he kept returning to.
- **Florence Wilson** (Worthing widow, Jun 1908; he left on 3 Jul with about £30) and
  **Sarah Freeman** (Oct 1909, as George Rose Smith; total taken put at about £400).
- **Beatrice "Bessie" Mundy** — married "Henry Williams" in Weymouth, 1910 (⚠ 26 Aug 1910 in
  some accounts). Money left by her father held in trust (accounts put it at about £2,500).
  Died **13 Jul 1912**, 80 High Street, **Herne Bay**, Kent.
- **Alice Burnham** — ⚠ a nurse, about 25, met at Southsea; married Smith late 1913 (⚠ Nov,
  Portsmouth; some say Oct). Life insurance about £500. Died **12 Dec 1913** in a Blackpool
  boarding house (⚠ 16 Regent Road, landlady Mrs Crossley).
- **Margaret Elizabeth Lofty**, 38 — ⚠ a clergyman's daughter; married "John Lloyd" at Bath,
  ⚠ 17 Dec 1914. Life policy about £700 (Yorkshire Insurance Company), taken out three days
  before the marriage. Died **18 Dec 1914**, 14 Bismarck Road (now Waterlow Road), **Highgate**.
- **Charles Burnham** — Alice's father. **Joseph Crossley** — Blackpool boarding-house owner;
  he and his wife Alice wrote to the police.
- **Divisional Detective Inspector Arthur Neil** (Kentish Town) — led the inquiry while also
  handling wartime duties.
- **Dr Frank French** (Herne Bay GP), **Dr Bates** (Highgate coroner's doctor, per Wikipedia).
- **Bernard Spilsbury** (1877–1947), Home Office pathologist; also gave evidence at Crippen
  (1910). Later reassessed: "very brilliant… but fallible… and very, very obstinate"
  (Sydney Smith); Keith Simpson and Burney & Pemberton (BMJ 2010) on "celebrity pathology".
- **Sir Edward Marshall Hall** — defence. **Archibald Bodkin** (later DPP), **Cecil Whiteley**,
  **Travers Humphreys** — prosecution. **Mr Justice Scrutton** — judge.

## Timeline
- **1898** marriage to Thornhill; **1901** two years' prison.
- **1908–1914** — seven bigamous marriages (Wikipedia). Pattern: marry, take savings and
  possessions, vanish; return to Edith Pegler with money.
- **1910** Weymouth — marries Bessie Mundy as Henry Williams. Unable to reach the trust money,
  he leaves her (⚠ with cash, and a letter blaming her). Gap of about two years.
- **1912** — chance meeting on the seafront at **Weston-super-Mare**; she takes him back.
  Herne Bay, 80 High Street, a house with no bath. ⚠ He buys a bath from a local ironmonger
  (accounts say he haggled the price down; ⚠ and that it was later returned unpaid).
- **8 Jul 1912** (⚠ "five days before her death" per Wikipedia) — wills made; hers in his
  favour.
- Smith takes Bessie to **Dr French**: she complains of headaches; Smith describes fits,
  suggesting epilepsy. (Wikipedia; Moore 2018.)
- **12 Jul 1912** — Williams says his wife has had another seizure; French visits.
- **13 Jul 1912**, morning — French is told she is dead; finds her in the bath, head under
  water, legs straight, feet above the water. No sign of violence; drowning put down to
  epilepsy. Inquest: misadventure/accident. Smith receives her money (accounts: about £2,500).
- **Late 1913** — marries Alice Burnham; insurance about £500. Blackpool, December. ⚠ Accounts
  say the couple turned down lodgings that had no bath. Wikipedia: "she took the lodgings in
  Blackpool only after Mr Smith inspected the bathtub."
- **12 Dec 1913** — Alice found dead in the bath by her husband. **13 Dec 1913** inquest
  report: accidental drowning.
- **17–18 Dec 1914** — Bath wedding as John Lloyd; Highgate lodgings; **18 Dec** will made
  making "John Lloyd" sole beneficiary, ~3 hours before her death; she withdraws her savings
  the same day (Wikipedia). Found dead in the bath by her husband and the landlady.
  ⚠ Widely repeated: the landlady heard splashing, then the harmonium in the sitting room
  playing "Nearer, My God, to Thee". Used in script only with "according to the landlady's
  later evidence, as it is usually told".
- **Before Christmas 1914** — *News of the World* reports Margaret Lloyd's death.
  Charles Burnham sees it. **3 Jan 1915** — Joseph Crossley writes to the Metropolitan Police,
  enclosing that cutting and the Blackpool inquest cutting.
- Neil visits Bismarck Road; finds the tub too small to believe an adult drowned in it
  by accident; tub three-quarters full; coroner's doctor saw only a tiny bruise above the
  left elbow.
- **12 Jan 1915** — Yorkshire Insurance enquiry; Neil asks the doctor to delay, then to issue
  a favourable report, and watches the solicitor's office.
- **1 Feb 1915** (Wikipedia) — "John Lloyd" appears; Neil asks if he is also George Smith;
  denial, then admission; held on suspicion of bigamy. ⚠ Wikipedia's later sentence gives
  "arrested on 15 February 1915"; script avoids the second date and says "early February".
- Margaret Lloyd exhumed; Spilsbury: tiny bruise, microscopic marks, little evidence of
  drowning, death apparently almost instantaneous; poison tests negative.
- **8 Feb 1915** — Herne Bay police send Neil a report of Bessie's death after reading the
  newspaper stories. Photographs confirm Henry Williams = John Lloyd = George Smith.
- Spilsbury examines Alice Burnham (Blackpool) and Bessie Mundy: in Bessie's case, goose
  flesh (cutis anserina) on the thigh — a sign of drowning. ⚠ Spilsbury article: she was
  "clutching a bar of soap" — contested; not used.
- The three tubs are brought to London (Kentish Town police station).
- **Spilsbury's reasoning:** Bessie 5 ft 7 in, tub about 5 ft. In the first, rigid phase of an
  epileptic fit the body stiffens and extends, pushing the upper body up the sloping end, out
  of the water; in the second phase the limbs jerk. Accidental submersion very unlikely.
  If the feet were suddenly lifted, the head would slide under; water rushing into nose and
  throat could cause sudden loss of consciousness — explaining no marks and few signs of
  drowning.
- **The experiment** (⚠ from Neil's own account): volunteers of similar build (Wikipedia:
  "experienced female divers"; often told as a swimmer/nurse) in a bathing costume; pushing
  them under caused an obvious struggle; when Neil suddenly pulled one volunteer's feet she
  slid under and went limp; it reportedly took him and a doctor about half an hour to revive
  her. She remembered only the rush of water. Hedge every detail.
- **23 Mar 1915** — formally charged with the three murders.
- **22 Jun 1915** — trial opens at the Old Bailey. Indictment: murder of Bessie Mundy.
  Scrutton admits the other two deaths as evidence of system over Marshall Hall's objection.
  ⚠ Widely cited: about 112 witnesses and 264 exhibits — hedge ("accounts put the number of
  witnesses at more than a hundred"). Smith does not give evidence (handwritten note to
  Marshall Hall survives — Commons image `counsel`). ⚠ Reported outbursts from the dock —
  not used.
- **1 Jul 1915** — jury out about **20 minutes**; guilty; sentence of death.
- Appeal on admission of "system" evidence dismissed (Lord Reading LCJ).
- **13 Aug 1915** — hanged at Maidstone Prison by John Ellis.

## Why it matters (the "first")
- Wikipedia: "one of the first cases in which striking similarities between connected crimes
  were used to prove guilt" — similar-fact ("system") evidence; precedent later invoked
  (e.g. committal of John Bodkin Adams, 1957, and criticised there by Devlin J).
- Established Spilsbury's public reputation as the foremost forensic pathologist; a turning
  point in reconstruction of how a death happened rather than only what caused it.
- Counterweight (for the legacy act): later criticism of Spilsbury's certainty and of
  celebrity pathology (Sydney Smith, Keith Simpson, Burney & Pemberton 2010). The conviction
  itself has never been overturned.
