## People
- **Thomas Farrow**, 71, manager of Chapman's Oil and Colour Shop, **34 Deptford High Street**,
  south-east London. Lived in the flat above with his wife **Ann Farrow**, 65.
- **William Jones**, 16 ⚠ (age per channel's earlier script; Wikipedia gives no age), shop assistant.
- **Louis Kidman**, worked in a nearby shop; helped Jones force the door.
- **George Chapman**, owner; collected the week's takings on Mondays.
- **Sergeant Albert Atkinson** — moved the cash box with his bare hands.
- **Chief Inspector Frederick Fox**; **Melville Macnaghten**, Assistant Commissioner (Crime),
  head of CID 1903–1913; had sat on the **Belper Committee** (1900), which recommended
  fingerprints over Bertillon's measurement system (anthropometry).
- **Detective Inspector Charles Stockley Collins**, head of the Fingerprint Bureau.
- **Sir Edward Henry**, Commissioner of the Metropolitan Police from 1903. Developed the Henry
  classification in Bengal (1896–97) with Sub-Inspectors **Azizul Haque** and **Hemchandra Bose**;
  founded the Fingerprint Bureau at Scotland Yard on **1 July 1901**.
- **Alfred Edward Stratton** (b. 1882) and **Albert Ernest Stratton** (b. 1884), brothers.
  Alfred had no criminal record but was "known to the police"; Albert was also known to police.
- **Annie Cromarty**, Alfred's girlfriend. **Kate Wade**, Albert's girlfriend.
- Witnesses: **Henry John Littlefield** (boxer ⚠ per Wikipedia), **Ellen Stanton**; milkman
  **Henry Alfred Jennings** (could not positively identify them).
- **William Gittings**, prison officer (jailer) where the brothers were held.
- Prosecutor **Richard Muir** (1857–1924) — relied on physical evidence; distrusted eyewitnesses
  unless they supported it. Later prosecuted Crippen (1910).
- Defence: **H. G. Rooth**, **Curtis Bennett**, **Harold Morris**.
- Defence experts: **Dr Henry Faulds** (Nature paper 1880; rival of Henry/Herschel/Galton);
  **Dr John George Garson** (anthropometry advocate; spoke against fingerprinting before Belper).
- Judge: **Mr Justice Channell**.

## Timeline
- **1880** — Faulds publishes first paper proposing forensic use of fingerprints (*Nature*).
- **1896–97** — Henry classification developed in Bengal; 1897 monograph *Classification and
  Uses of Finger Prints*.
- **1900** — Belper Committee recommends fingerprints.
- **1 Jul 1901** — Fingerprint Bureau, Scotland Yard.
- **1902** — Harry Jackson convicted of burglary on fingerprint evidence.
- **Mon 27 Mar 1905, ~7:30 am** — two men seen leaving the shop: one in a dark brown suit and
  cap, the other in a dark blue serge suit and bowler hat.
- **8:30 am** — Jones finds shop closed and shuttered; sees chairs knocked over through window.
  With Kidman forces entry. Thomas Farrow dead; Ann Farrow unconscious in bed upstairs; both
  beaten. Taken to hospital.
- Scene: no forced entry; empty cash box (accounts put contents at about **£13** ⚠); two black
  masks made from stocking tops; victims in night clothes. Police reconstruction ⚠ (inference):
  Farrow opened the door half-awake, was attacked, fought back, was attacked again; Mrs Farrow
  attacked upstairs; attackers washed hands in a basin.
- Macnaghten examines cash box; greasy smudge on underside of inner tray. Picks it up with a
  handkerchief, wrapped in paper, to the Bureau.
- Collins: print left in sweat, right thumb probably; excludes Farrows and Atkinson; no match in
  80,000–90,000 sets on file.
- **31 Mar** — Ann Farrow dies without regaining consciousness.
- Littlefield and Stanton identify Alfred as the man in the brown suit. Cromarty: Alfred got rid
  of his brown coat, changed shoes the day after, asked her for old stockings.
- **2 Apr (Sun)** — Alfred arrested at the King of Prussia pub. **3 Apr** — Albert arrested on
  the High Street. Charged at Greenwich police station, Blackheath Road; fingerprinted.
- £4 recovered, buried near a local waterworks (tip from Cromarty).
- Collins: cash-box print matches **Alfred's right thumb**. Items now in the Crime Museum,
  New Scotland Yard.
- **5–6 May 1905** — trial at the Old Bailey. Over 40 prosecution witnesses.
  - Alfred testifies: Albert woke him about 2:30 am wanting money for lodgings; slept on the
    floor; both stayed until 9. The £4: boxing winnings, buried three weeks before.
  - Gittings: Albert reportedly said "I reckon he will get strung up and I shall get about ten
    years… He has led me into this."
  - Collins shows the jury enlargements; **up to twelve points of agreement**; demonstrates
    effect of pressure at a juror's request.
  - Garson says the prints do not agree. Muir produces two letters written by Garson **on the
    same day**, one to the Director of Public Prosecutions, one to the defence solicitor, each
    offering to give evidence ⚠ (accounts differ on wording: "for either side"/"to the highest
    bidder"). Garson: "I am an independent witness." Justice Channell: "absolutely untrustworthy".
  - Defence does not call Faulds.
  - Jury out a little over two hours ⚠ (Wikipedia; some accounts say about two hours). Guilty.
    Sentenced to death **6 May 1905**.
- **23 May 1905, 9 am** — both hanged at HM Prison Wandsworth.

## The "first"
First murder conviction in Britain in which fingerprint evidence was central. (Not the first
fingerprint conviction: Harry Jackson, burglary, 1902.)
