## People
- **Hawley Harvey Crippen** (11 Sep 1862 – 23 Nov 1910). Born Coldwater, Michigan. Homeopath;
  graduated Cleveland Homeopathic Medical College 1884. His US qualifications did not allow
  him to practise as a doctor in the UK. Sold patent medicines; managed the Drouet Institute
  for the Deaf. First wife Charlotte died of a stroke in 1892; son Hawley Otto raised by
  Crippen's parents in California.
- **Cora Crippen** — born Kunigunde Mackamotski, known as Corrine "Cora" Turner; music hall
  singer under the stage name **Belle Elmore**. Married Crippen 1894 (New York). Moved to
  London 1897, when Crippen came to run Munyon's branch office.
- **Ethel Le Neve** (born Ethel Clara Neave, 21 Jan 1883, Diss, Norfolk – 9 Aug 1967). Typist,
  hired by Crippen in 1900. Affair by 1905; his mistress by 1908.
- **Chief Inspector Walter Dew** (1863–1947), Scotland Yard. Earlier a Whitechapel detective
  during the 1888 Ripper murders. Memoir *I Caught Crippen* (1938), which he admitted
  contains errors.
- **Captain Henry George Kendall** (1874–1965), master of Canadian Pacific's SS *Montrose*.
  Had worked with Marconi on ship-to-shore radio before his first command in 1908.
- **Lawrence Ernest Hughes** — the *Montrose*'s wireless telegraphist.
- **William Willcox** — senior scientific analyst to the Home Office; found hyoscine.
- **Bernard Spilsbury** — pathologist; gave evidence on the scar.
- Cora's friends who pushed the police: strongwoman **Kate "Vulcana" Williams**; actress
  **Lil Hawthorne** and her husband/manager **John Nash**, who knew Superintendent
  **Frank Froest**.

## Timeline
- **31 Jan 1910** — dinner party at **39 Hilldrop Crescent**, Camden Road, Holloway. Cora is
  not seen again.
- After that, Crippen says she has gone back to America; later, that she has died and been
  cremated in California. Ethel moves in and wears Cora's clothes and jewellery.
- Cora's friends grow suspicious; police take it seriously after Nash and Hawthorne go to
  Froest.
- **8 Jul 1910** — Dew questions Crippen. Crippen admits the death story was invented: Cora,
  he says, left him for a music hall actor, **Bruce Miller**, and he lied to avoid
  embarrassment. Dew searches the house, finds nothing, and is satisfied.
- **Next day** — Crippen and Ethel flee: Brussels (one night in a hotel), then Antwerp. Ethel
  dressed as a boy; Crippen shaves his moustache. They travel as "Mr Robinson" and his son.
- **20 Jul** — board the *Montrose* at Antwerp, bound for Quebec.
- Their disappearance prompts further searches. **12–13 Jul**, fourth and final search:
  human remains under the brick floor of the coal cellar. Head, limbs and skeleton were
  never recovered. Hair curlers, bleached hair, and a fragment of a pyjama top were found
  with them.
- Willcox finds **hyoscine** (scopolamine). Crippen had bought hyoscine from a local chemist
  before Cora disappeared.
- **16 Jul** — warrants for Crippen and Le Neve.
- At sea: Kendall notices that "Mr Robinson" resembles the wanted doctor and that the boy's
  build and manner look feminine. ⚠ That the pair were "unusually affectionate" is reported.
- Just before leaving transmitter range, Kendall has Hughes send the telegram (verbatim):
  > "Have strong suspicions that Crippen London cellar murderer and accomplice are among
  > saloon passengers. Mustache taken off growing beard. Accomplice dressed as boy. Manner
  > and build undoubtedly a girl."
  Sent to the line's Liverpool office, passed to Scotland Yard. Widely described as **the
  first time wireless telegraphy was used to catch a criminal**.
- ⚠ "Had Crippen travelled third class, he would probably have escaped Kendall's notice."
  This is a judgement in the sources, not a fact.
- Dew sails from Liverpool on the faster White Star liner **SS *Laurentic***. By his own
  account he lands on **29 Jul**, two days ahead of the *Montrose*.
- **31 Jul 1910** — as the *Montrose* enters the St Lawrence near **Father Point**, Dew boards
  disguised as a pilot. Kendall invites Crippen to meet the pilots.
  > Dew: "Good morning, Dr Crippen. Do you know me? I'm Chief Inspector Dew from Scotland
  > Yard."
  > Crippen, after a pause: "Thank God it's over. The suspense has been too great. I couldn't
  > stand it any longer."
  He holds out his wrists. A Canadian officer makes the arrest; Le Neve is arrested soon
  after. (Canada was a dominion, so no extradition fight. Both waived extradition rights.)
- **27 Aug** — they land at Liverpool aboard the *Megantic*.
- **18 Oct 1910** — trial at the Old Bailey before Lord Chief Justice **Lord Alverstone**,
  four days. Defence (Alfred Tobin): Cora fled to America; the remains predate the Crippens'
  time in the house. Spilsbury identifies an abdominal scar consistent with Cora's medical
  history; the defence says it is a fold of tissue (hair follicles). Pyjama fragment with a
  **Jones Bros.** label — a company witness said that pattern was not sold before 1908, so it
  dates to the Crippens' time in the house.
- Jury: **guilty after 27 minutes**. Le Neve, tried separately as an accessory after the fact,
  is **acquitted**.
- **23 Nov 1910, 9 am** — Crippen hanged at Pentonville by John Ellis. At his request a
  photograph of Ethel was buried with him.
- Le Neve: Toronto, then back to London as "Ethel Harvey"; married Stanley Smith in 1915; died
  in Croydon in 1967.

## Legacy and dispute
- First criminal caught with the help of wireless telegraphy. Newspapers called it "the most
  sensational criminal capture of the century". Dew resigned in 1910 and later sued nine
  newspapers for libel.
- ⚠ **2007**: David Foran's Michigan State team said mitochondrial DNA from a slide of the
  cellar remains did not match Cora's living relatives, and that a Y-chromosome test showed
  the tissue was male (published in *J Forensic Sciences*, 2010). This was disputed: one
  century-old slide, a new technique. **The Criminal Cases Review Commission (2009) did not
  refer the case** to the Court of Appeal. On screen: report the claim and the dispute; do
  not say Crippen was innocent.
- ⚠ Motive: never given by Crippen. Theories (e.g. accidental overdose — Marshall Hall)
  are speculation. Mention at most once, labelled as theory.

## Respect line
Cora Crippen — Belle Elmore, a music hall singer whose friends refused to let her
disappearance go unasked. They were the reason anyone looked.
