## People
- **PC George William Gutteridge** (1891–1927), born Downham Market, Norfolk. Joined Essex
  Police 1910 (PC 489); Southend, Romford, Grays. Machine Gun Corps 1918; back in the police 1919.
  From March 1922 the village constable of **Stapleford Abbotts**, between Ongar and Romford.
  Lived at 2 Towneley Cottages with his wife **Rose Annette Emmerline** and two children,
  aged 12 and 4 (Wikipedia, citing Police Journal 1983). Age 36 at death (b. 1891).
- **Frederick Guy Browne** (b. Catford, 1881 per NBT l.725; 1883 per Wikipedia ⚠). Bicycle
  repairer turned bicycle and then car thief; convictions incl. carrying a revolver (1910).
  Served in Parkhurst and Dartmoor; released March 1927. Ran a garage at Northcote Road,
  Lavender Hill, near Clapham Junction. Very tall (6 ft 7 in reported ⚠).
- **William Henry Kennedy** (b. Ayrshire, 1891). Petty criminal with many aliases; deserted from
  the army more than once. Browne's handyman at the garage in 1927. Married on 18 January 1928.
- **PC Sydney James Taylor** — neighbouring constable (Lambourne End); met Gutteridge at Howe
  Green at about 3 am on 27 Sep 1927.
- **William Alec Ward** — postman who found the body about 6 am.
- **Dr Edward Lovell** of Billericay — owner of the stolen Morris Cowley, TW 6120.
- **Chief Inspector James Berrett**, Scotland Yard, in charge.
- **Sir Wyndham Childs**, Assistant Commissioner (CID): fixed on the unusual cartridge (NBT l.2017).
- Experts at trial: **George Henry Ibbitson** (Royal Arsenal, Woolwich), **Robert Churchill**
  (gunmaker, Leicester Square), **William Fox** (chief examiner of small arms, Royal Small Arms
  Factory, Enfield Lock), **George Henry Perry** (War Department chemist, Woolwich).
- **Mr Justice Avory** (judge); **Sir Boyd Merriman**, Solicitor-General, for the Crown.
- **Detective Sergeant William Guthrie Mattinson**, Liverpool — Kennedy tried to shoot him.

## Timeline
- **26 Sep 1927, 11 pm** — Gutteridge leaves home for night duty, wearing his cape.
- **27 Sep, ~3 am** — meets PC Taylor at Howe Green; they part ~3.25.
- Same night — Dr Lovell's Morris Cowley is stolen from his garage in Billericay (Kennedy's
  statement: they first meant to take another car, were disturbed, took the Morris).
- **~3.30-4 am** — on the road between Howe Green and Stapleford Abbotts, Gutteridge signals
  the car to stop. Kennedy's statement (NBT l.1740-1770): the constable asks where they come
  from, asks Browne for a licence; Browne has none; Kennedy says the car is his; the constable
  shines his torch in both their faces, asks the number, Kennedy gives "T.W.6120"; "Very well.
  I'll take particulars"; he takes out his notebook; two shots; he falls by the bank.
  Kennedy: "What have you done?" Browne: "Get out quick." Kennedy says he went round to the
  man; Browne said he would "finish" him; Kennedy: "For God's sake don't shoot any more, the
  man's dying." "The policeman's eyes were open, and Browne, addressing him, said, 'What are
  you looking at me like that for?' and, stooping down, shot him at close range through both
  eyes." ⚠ This account is **only Kennedy's**, and in law it was evidence against Kennedy alone
  (NBT l.3068-3071; magistrate and judge both ruled so, l.1951).
- Kennedy says Browne gave him the revolver to reload; "in my excitement I dropped an empty
  shell in the car" (NBT l.1772-1775). They drove through fog via Buckhurst Hill, Bow and the
  Elephant and Castle, crashed lightly, abandoned the car in a cul-de-sac in Brixton (Foxley
  Road, Stockwell area) and took a tram to the garage.
- **~6 am** — postman Ward finds Gutteridge semi-sitting against the bank, legs in the road,
  pencil in his right hand, notebook and helmet beside him (NBT, Ward's evidence l.3820-3836).
  He fetches Mr Perritt of Rose Cottage. ⚠ A bus driver, Warren, reportedly could not get the
  operator to connect a call from a public box without payment (Billericay history society,
  via Wikipedia) — later linked by some writers to the case for the 999 service (introduced
  1937). Treat as "it has been claimed".
- Wounds: two shots through the left cheek, one through each eye (Dr Woodhouse; NBT). Two
  bullets were found in the road where he lay, so the last shots were fired as he lay on the
  ground (NBT l.3123-3127).
- **Same day** — the Morris Cowley is found in Brixton; blood on the running board; an empty
  **.455 cartridge case marked "R.L. IV"** (Royal Laboratory, Woolwich, Mark IV) under a seat.
- **30 Sep** — inquest: murder by person or persons unknown.
- Childs: Mark IV cartridges withdrawn from the army in 1914 (NBT l.2019) ⚠ (Wikipedia: not
  produced since 1913); one eye shot propelled by **black powder**, not used in service
  ammunition since 1894 (NBT l.2022) — so the killer had old, mixed ammunition.
- Autumn 1927 — revolver after revolver is tested; none matches (NBT l.2030).
- ⚠ **14 Nov 1927, Sheffield** (Sillitoe memoir, 1955): a minor collision; a crook named
  Charles Currie, a passenger, later names the driver as Browne and links him to the murder.
  Memoir source: say "according to the Sheffield chief constable's own memoir".
- **20 Jan 1928** — Browne arrested at his Clapham Junction garage on a car-theft charge.
  Loaded Webley in the car, a second Webley in a hidden compartment behind the driver's seat
  (Bevis's evidence), more weapons, R.L. Mark IV cartridges, stolen goods including medical
  instruments (forceps) of the kind Dr Lovell lost (NBT l.672-679; Wikipedia).
- **25-26 Jan 1928, Liverpool, Copperas Hill** — Kennedy arrested. He points an automatic pistol
  at DS Mattinson: "Stand back, or I'll shoot you"; pulls the trigger; the safety catch is on
  (NBT l.3311-3315). Later to Mattinson: "I'm sorry. I've no grudge against the police, but you
  should be in Heaven by now, and there was one for me." (l.3324-3327)
- **26/27 Jan** — at Scotland Yard, after his wife's visit (Mrs Kennedy: "Why, you didn't murder
  him, did you?" — "No, I didn't, but I was there and know who did." NBT l.3352-3355), Kennedy
  makes his statement blaming Browne. Defence later claimed it was not voluntary (tired,
  unfed) — rejected.
- **23-27 Apr 1928, Old Bailey**, Mr Justice Avory. Application for separate trials refused.
- Firearms evidence (NBT l.7380-7590):
  - Ibbitson: an unopened packet of 12 R.L. Mark IV cartridges "packed in 1913"; the case from
    the car was stamped with the same tool; fired from that revolver.
  - Churchill: "the cap of the cartridge case takes the imprint of the breech shield of the
    revolver, and under microscope examination it is possible for me to see that this
    particular cartridge was fired from this Webley revolver, and could not have been fired from
    any other revolver." Test shots into a **pigskin target** at 3, 6 and 12 inches: two cheek
    shots cordite (little marking), right-eye shot black powder (tattooing).
  - Fox: "Not only give an opinion — I do not give an opinion: I say that case was fired from
    that revolver." Avory: "You mean to say that without doubt?" — "Absolutely, my lord."
    Two overlapping indentations on the breech shield at **twelve o'clock**, made by a
    succession of blows; matching embossing on the copper cap. "[The jury being handed a
    microscope in order to examine the cartridge.]" He searched the stock of revolvers awaiting
    repair (number illegible in OCR ⚠, reportedly about 1,300+) and found none marked alike.
  - Perry: photographic enlargements of breech marks shown to the jury.
- **NBT introduction (l.2055-2082):** "no two natural or man-made objects are ever precisely the
  same"; breech shields are finished "by hand with the use of a file"; "Why not call it a
  'gun print'?"; "had Browne got rid of his pistol, he would probably still be alive" and "first
  catch your gun!"
- **27 Apr 1928** — jury out 12.52 to 3.10 (2 h 18 min). Both guilty. Browne: "the jury have had
  stuff given to them that is not genuine… my conscience is clear." Kennedy: "it is
  pre-ordained, it was fate… I am not afraid to die" (NBT l.12730-12775).
- Appeal dismissed (separate trials, summing up).
- **31 May 1928, 9 am** — Browne hanged at Pentonville, Kennedy at Wandsworth, at the same hour.
- Gutteridge buried at Warley; memorial at the spot; road later named **Gutteridge Lane**.
  Revolver and bullets: Essex Police Museum / Met Crime Museum.

## Contested / interpretation (for the analysis passages)
1. **The eyes.** A legend grew that the eyes were shot because of the old belief (optography,
   Kühne 1870s-80s) that a dead person's retina keeps the last image seen. ⚠ No trial witness
   says so. The only account is Kennedy's, which gives Browne's words "What are you looking at
   me like that for?" — a reason of a different kind. ExecutedToday calls the superstition
   "conceivably" the motive. So: the record supports the words, not the superstition.
2. **Who fired.** Browne denied being there at all (alibi: his wife). Kennedy's statement was
   not evidence against Browne. So what convicted Browne in law was the gun, the cartridges and
   the car evidence; the statement convicted Kennedy (joint enterprise). NBT editor's quoted
   observer doubted Browne fired the eye shots (l.716-717) ⚠ opinion. Kennedy's solicitor later
   claimed Browne was not guilty (book synopsis, *The Long Drop*) ⚠ unverified. 2020 BBC review
   (Judge Radford) found the verdict safe.
3. **The 999 link.** Popular claim; the 999 service started in London in 1937 after a 1935
   fire in Wimpole Street. Only say "the delay has been linked by some writers".
4. **"First".** Firearms identification had been used in British courts before (e.g. Churchill's
   earlier work), so the safe phrasing is: the case that made "gun prints" famous in Britain,
   and the trial at which the jury itself was handed the microscope. Not "the first ballistics
   case".
